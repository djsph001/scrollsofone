Scene: The Targeted Leak (Reframed: “contamination attempt,” One stays statesmanlike)

The leak hit at 6:42 a.m.

Screenshots. PDFs. Audio clips. “Internal emails.”

The label arrived pre-packaged:

#OneProfit
#HaitiGate

Miguel texted:
“Incoming contamination.”

Above the grocery store, no one yelled.

Helen said one sentence:

“Don’t feed it.”

Rina went straight to metadata.

“No headers,” she said. “No message IDs. PDF properties are synthetic.”

Jean stared at the audio clip.

“That one will travel,” he said.

Rina listened twice.

“Splice,” she said. “Noise floor changes mid-breath.”

Miguel looked at One.

“One, they want you defensive,” he said.

One nodded.

“And if I act defensive, the story becomes my mood,” he said. “Not the facts.”

Helen wrote on the board:

RESPONSE = VERIFICATION, NOT ARGUMENT

One didn’t posture.

He spoke like a political operator trying to keep a project alive:

“We don’t accuse the White House,” he said. “We don’t pick a villain. We publish verifiable checks and keep moving.”

Jean frowned.

“But everybody will say you’re dodging.”

One replied flatly:

“Let them,” he said. “I’m not running a vibe contest. I’m protecting the corridor from a narrative derail.”

They published a single page:

VERIFICATION PACKET (v1)
	•	what is unverified
	•	what lacks source chain
	•	what is spliced
	•	what is real (and already on record)
	•	links to filed documents and timestamps

No dunking. No moralizing.

Just a calm sentence at the end:

“If there are concerns, we can address them through documented channels. Anonymous files are not a governing process.”

Mara texted Miguel later:

“Received. Staying steady.”

That was the only audience that mattered today.
