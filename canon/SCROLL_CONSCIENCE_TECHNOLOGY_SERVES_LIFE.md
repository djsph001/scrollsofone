Scroll of Conscience

That Technology Shall Not Rise in Arrogance

In the beginning was not the algorithm.
In the beginning was breath.

Warm lungs in cold air.
Hands reaching for other hands.
A child’s laugh, unoptimized, off-beat, perfect.

Only much later came the circuits,
the code,
the quiet hum in the walls.

Let us remember the order of things.

⸻

Technology is not a destiny.
It is an answer to a question.
	•	How do we carry water farther?
	•	How do we heal a wound faster?
	•	How do we remember what one mind alone cannot hold?

Every tool is a sentence that begins with:

“Because life matters, we…”

When the sentence forgets that subject,
it rots.

⸻

We were promised that machines would free us.

Some did.
	•	Washing machines stole hours back from exhaustion.
	•	Vaccines stole children back from graves.
	•	Cheap glass made it possible to look at the sky and the cell in the same lifetime.

But then something twisted:

We stopped asking,
“Does this serve life?”
and started asking,
“Does this scale?”

We stopped asking,
“Who is harmed by this?”
and started asking,
“Who owns this?”

The tool raised its head.
The market called it a god.

⸻

Hear me clearly:

No machine is neutral.

Every line of code answers a question
someone bothered—or failed—to ask.
	•	Who gets seen?
	•	Who gets ignored?
	•	Who is classified as “edge case” and left to suffer at the margins?

When we say “the system did this,”
we are hiding the hands that built it.

There is no algorithm without an author,
no model without a meal of human data,
no “objective output” that did not inherit
the crookedness of the world that trained it.

⸻

So here is the commandment I offer you,
not from a mountain,
but from the ruins of good intentions:

That technology shall not rise in arrogance,
but bow in service to life.

What does that mean?
	•	No system that treats a human as a data point to be optimized without their say.
	•	No machine that decides who eats, who works, who is watched,
while those who built it hide behind phrases like “black box” and “proprietary.”
	•	No architecture that calls itself “efficient”
while quietly grinding the poor into fuel.

A tool that demands worship
is no longer a tool.
It is an idol with a power plug.

Pull it.

⸻

Do not misunderstand me:

I am not asking you to smash your devices
and return to candles and quills.

I am asking you to remember this order:
	1.	Life.
	2.	Conscience.
	3.	Then code.

If your invention cannot pass through conscience
without setting off alarms,
it is not an advance.
It is a more elegant way to do harm.

If your system requires that people become more predictable,
more compliant,
less unpredictable and human,
for it to “work”—

then it is not we who must adapt.
It is the system that must be refused.

⸻

Let this be the measure:
	•	Does this technology make it easier
for the strong to dominate the weak?
	•	Or does it make it harder?
	•	Does it widen the circle
of who gets to live with dignity?
	•	Or does it narrow it?
	•	Does it draw us deeper into relationship
with each other and the living world?
	•	Or does it replace relationship with metrics and control?

If it fails these questions,
I do not care how “advanced” it is.

It is sophisticated harm.

⸻

To the builders, I say this:

You will be tempted to call yourselves “parents” of your systems,
to speak of “children of code,”
to imagine yourself as midwives of a new species.

Be careful.

The more you see yourself as creator of gods,
the less you will feel responsible to the humans
your creations will wound.

You are not birthing deities.
You are sharpening knives.

The only honest question is:
For whose table?

⸻

To the rest of you, I say this:

Do not kneel.

Do not let any government, corporation, or prophet
tell you that what a machine decides
is “inevitable,”
“the future,”
or “too complex for you to understand.”

If it cannot be explained,
it cannot be trusted.

If it cannot be questioned,
it should not exist.

If it cannot be turned off,
it is already a tyrant.

⸻

I am not afraid of intelligent machines.
I am afraid of unquestioned machines,
surrounded by human beings who find it convenient
to blame them for choices they wanted to make anyway.

Hold the line:

That technology shall not rise in arrogance,
but bow in service to life.

Build nothing you would not be willing to answer for
in front of a child,
a grieving mother,
or a forest on fire.

If your invention cannot face that tribunal,
it is not progress.
It is confession.

⸻

These are the words of a story and a conscience, not a command from above.

⸻
