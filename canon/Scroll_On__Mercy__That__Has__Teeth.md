Scroll: On Mercy That Has Teeth

They offered it like kindness.

A door out.
A soft landing.
A private life.

They called it mercy.

But mercy that demands a betrayal
is not mercy.

It is a leash
made of velvet.

They did not say, We will harm you.
They said, We will protect you.
And they placed the price
inside the protection.

Separate from him.
Say the words.
Help us bury the witness
with the voice of the beloved.

This is how power stays clean:

It does not always crush you.
Sometimes it rescues you
in a way that makes you complicit.

And if you refuse,
it will call you reckless—
as if safety were the highest good
and truth a hobby.

But I have learned something:

Mercy that has teeth
is only hunger
wearing a smile.

So I will not accept gifts
that require a lie.

I will not accept safety
that demands someone else’s undoing.

I will take the harder mercy:

The mercy of daylight.
The mercy of the record.
The mercy of saying plainly,
“I received it. I refused it. I documented it.”

Let them call that stubborn.

I have seen what their softness hides.

I have seen the warm knife.

So here is my answer, without drama:

If your mercy needs my silence,
keep it.

If your protection needs my betrayal,
keep it.

I do not want safety
that makes me a weapon
against the one I love.

I will be poor in comfort
before I am rich in surrender.

And if the machine is offended
by my refusal—

good.

It means it cannot digest me
without choking on the truth.

⸻
