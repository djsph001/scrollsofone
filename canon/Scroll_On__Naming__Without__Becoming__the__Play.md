Scroll: On Naming Without Becoming the Play

There is a temptation
to shout the name
as if volume were proof.

There is a temptation
to turn one man into a monster
so we can feel clean
while the system remains untouched.

But I have learned:

A name is not the point.
A structure is.

So I will not name him like a curse.
I will name him like a coordinate.

Not to summon a crowd,
but to summon a question.

Not to ignite a religion,
but to ignite a record.

Because the danger is not only a man—
it is the machinery around the man
that rewards the lie,
protects the smear,
and calls coercion “care.”

So here is my vow:

I will not become the performance
they need me to be.

I will not become the rival prophet
that makes their story exciting.

I will not become the counter-king
that keeps the cycle alive.

I will become the witness
who stays ordinary enough
to be believed.

Ask me under oath.
Ask me with definitions.
Ask me with dates.

And I will answer.

Not with hate,
but with proof.

Not with spectacle,
but with the quiet violence
of verification.

Because when the record is strong,
the name does not need to be shouted.

It only needs to be placed
where it cannot be erased.
