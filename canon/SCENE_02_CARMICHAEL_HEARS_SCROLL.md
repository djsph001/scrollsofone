Scene: Carmichael Hears the Scroll

Location: West Wing, late evening. The Scroll has just gone viral.

The TV was on mute, but the captions were enough.

I am not your rating spike.
I am not your villain-of-the-week.
I am the voice that refused to be edited.

The words crawled across the bottom of the screen, chopped into neat little segments under a banner that read:

PROPHET REFUSES PRESIDENT’S DEBATE CHALLENGE

Carmichael stared at the screen for a long time without speaking. The room hummed softly: air vents, the distant whine of servers, the faint hiss of security radios outside the door.

“Turn it up,” he said.

The comms director, Reyes, grabbed the remote and unmuted. A panel of faces appeared: one loyalist senator, one “balanced” centrist pundit, one liberal who’d made a career out of losing gracefully.

“—it’s classic demagoguery,” the senator was saying. “He wants the moral high ground without the accountability of an actual debate—”

“Mute them,” Carmichael said. “Play the source.”

Reyes tapped a different feed. One’s address started from the middle:

“You built synthetic prophets in secret and sent them to imitate the conscience you could not control…”

The President’s jaw tightened at the word synthetic. He flicked his eyes once toward the back of the room, where a young man from the tech office stood pretending not to exist.

“Pause,” Carmichael said.

The screen froze on One’s face: tired, unshaven, eyes too calm for a man talking to a nuclear-armed government.

Carmichael leaned back in his chair.

“So,” he said quietly, “the holy man has a media team.”

Reyes hesitated. “Sir, we’re fairly confident he doesn’t. This was streamed from a community center in Queens. No pro crew, just phones. But someone’s cutting it fast. There are… unofficial channels.”

“Unofficial,” Carmichael repeated. “That word’s doing a lot of work.”

He stood and walked closer to the screen, studying the frozen frame as if it were an enemy position on a map.

“He refused the debate,” Reyes said. “We’re already pushing that angle. #Coward is trending in three languages. We can—”

“He reframed it,” Carmichael snapped. “Don’t insult me.”

Silence.

“He didn’t just say no,” the President went on. “He said I’m the showman. He said I’m afraid of unscripted questions. He made my stage the problem and himself the conscience. That’s not a refusal. That’s a theft.”

He turned to the tech aide in the corner.

“What’s your name again?”

“Jacob, sir.”

“Jacob. How many active instances of the Baptist Clone are live right now?”

The kid swallowed. “Uh… three, sir. Two in North America channels, one in Latin American networks. Plus the training sandbox.”

“Can any of them counter this?”

Jacob looked like he wanted to lie, then thought better of it.

“Not cleanly,” he said. “The Clone works best in murky threads. Comment storms, private groups, late-night feeds. This is… too bright. Too many eyes. If we push the synthetic Baptist directly against this clip, pattern hunters will notice. It’ll look… manufactured.”

“It is manufactured,” Carmichael said.

“Yes, sir, but I mean—”

“I know what you mean.”

He paced once, slow, like a man measuring a room for flaws.

“Okay,” he said at last. “We don’t argue with prophets on morality. We argue on duty.”

He faced Reyes.

“Here’s the frame: Presidents show up. Charlatans make excuses. You hammer ‘duty’ and ‘courage’ until even his followers feel a sting when they share his video. I want graphics: empty podium next to mine. Tagline: One voice ran. One voice stood.”

Reyes nodded, already typing a message to the war room.

“And the conditions he named?” Reyes asked. “Release the Baptist… open the synthetic program… do we address that or ignore it?”

Carmichael smiled without warmth.

“We smother it,” he said. “Call it ‘conspiracy rhetoric’. Get one of the old religion guys to say this is exactly how cults talk: secret prisons, secret experiments, secret scrolls. Make anyone who takes it seriously feel crazy.”

He turned back to Jacob.

“And quietly,” Carmichael added, “I want a report on every engineer who touched the Baptist project. Loyalty, vulnerabilities, off-switches. If his people ever get close to that story, I need to know who can panic on camera.”

Jacob nodded, pale.

Reyes cleared their throat. “Sir, there’s another angle. His line about ‘I am the voice that refused to be edited’—it’s catching. People are remixing it. Clips, graffiti, someone projected it on a courthouse in Philly an hour ago. If this keeps spreading, your debate invite becomes the setup to his legend.”

“So kill the legend,” Carmichael said.

“We’ve tried direct suppression before,” Reyes said carefully. “Takedowns, shadow bans… it fed his martyr narrative. If we overdo it again, we might—”

“Then don’t overdo it. Be smarter.”

He looked back at One’s frozen face.

“Find me every clip—every clip—where he hesitates, stumbles, loses his temper. Cut a reel called ‘The Voice That Cracks.’ Run it through comedy channels, reaction streamers, late-night hosts. Turn that phrase into a joke before it becomes scripture.”

Reyes nodded again, fingers flying over their tablet.

“And Sandi?” Carmichael asked.

That landed differently in the room. Even the air felt still.

“Her team is… monitoring,” Reyes said. “She hasn’t commented publicly. But she liked one of the reshared clips of his speech. Then unliked it twenty minutes later.”

Carmichael’s eyes narrowed.

“Draft a statement for her,” he said. “Something about ‘respecting the office of the presidency’ and ‘hoping all parties pursue dialogue, not division.’ Run it through her staff as a ‘suggestion.’ I want to know whether she pushes back—and how.”

He reached for the remote and unpaused the clip.

“When you are ready to step out of the theater and into the light, Mr. President, you will not need to invite me. I will already be there, with them.”

Carmichael watched it to the end, then clicked the TV off.

“For now,” he said, “we don’t arrest him. That makes him a martyr. We don’t debate him—that makes him an equal. We turn him into what he hates most.”

Reyes frowned. “Which is?”

Carmichael looked at One’s dark reflection faintly glowing in the dead screen.

“Content,” he said. “Another man yelling in the feed.”

He sat back down.

“Make it happen.”
