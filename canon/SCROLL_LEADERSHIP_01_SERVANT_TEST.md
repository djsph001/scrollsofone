# Scroll of Leadership  
## The Servant Test

They will ask you to trust them.

They will stand on stages lit like altars,  
speak in cadences borrowed from prophets,  
promise you safety, prosperity, greatness,  
and frame every dissent as betrayal.

This scroll is a reminder of the oldest test:

**A leader worth trusting does not ask to be worshiped.**

---

I have watched them rise, these men and women who confuse authority with divinity.

They arrive humble — or pretending to be —  
speaking the language of the forgotten,  
kissing babies, quoting scripture, wearing the uniform of the ordinary.

Then the cameras multiply.  
Then the crowds chant.  
Then someone whispers in their ear:  
*"You are the only one who can save us."*

And something shifts.

The servant becomes the idol.  
The representative becomes the represented.  
The public trust becomes personal empire.

This is not about left or right.  
This is about up and down:

- those who lead from below, lifting others, and  
- those who lead from above, demanding others lift them.

---

Here is the test I learned too late and now offer you:

**The worthy leader washes feet before claiming thrones.**

Not as performance.  
Not as strategy.  
As instinct.

- They know the names of those who clean the halls after the speeches.  
- They listen longer than they speak.  
- They are unbothered by being corrected in public.  
- They do not collect loyalists — they build capacity in others to lead without them.

They are not performing humility.  
They *are* humble, the way water is wet:  
not as a costume, but as composition.

---

Beware the leader who:

- speaks only in certainties,  
- tolerates no criticism,  
- treats questions as insults,  
- believes their survival is the same as the nation's survival,  
- claims only they can decode the will of God or history or the people.

That is not leadership.  
That is **the shell of leadership wrapped around a will to dominate.**

You will know them by what they cannot tolerate:

- being wrong,  
- being ignored,  
- being replaced.

True servants can imagine the world continuing beautifully without them.  
Idols cannot.

---

I am not innocent of this trap.

I have stood in rooms where people looked at me  
as if I held answers I did not have.

I have felt the drug of being needed,  
the seduction of being called indispensable,  
the terrible warmth of a crowd saying *yes* in unison.

It is easy to believe you are the hinge of history  
when everyone around you keeps oiling your ego.

So I return, again and again, to one question:

**Am I building something that can survive without me?**

If the answer is no,  
I am not leading.  
I am **occupying**.

---

The worthy leader:

- **Teaches others to lead** — does not hoard knowledge or access.  
- **Steps aside when their time is done** — does not cling to the chair like it is oxygen.  
- **Admits error publicly** — does not blame subordinates, the media, or "misunderstanding."  
- **Protects the weak from the strong** — not just in rhetoric, but in resource allocation, in law, in culture.  
- **Sees power as responsibility, not reward** — never forgets that authority is borrowed from the people it serves.

If they begin to speak of themselves in mythic terms,  
if they frame opposition as evil rather than disagreement,  
if they believe their removal would be catastrophe rather than transition—

**They have failed the test.**

---

Mark this:

The idol-leader will always have an excuse for why democracy must wait,  
why transparency is dangerous,  
why dissent is treason,  
why **this time** the rules must bend for the sake of survival.

They will tell you:  
*"Trust me now. Judge me later. I alone can fix this."*

Do not believe them.

That sentence is the enemy of every republic,  
every council,  
every attempt by human beings to govern themselves  
without bowing to a single throat.

---

If you are building something worth preserving,  
build it so that **no one person is the keystone.**

Not me.  
Not them.  
Not anyone.

The system that depends on one irreplaceable savior  
is not a system.  
It is a **hostage situation dressed as governance.**

---

I will not tell you who to follow.

I will tell you what to look for:

- Do they empower you, or do they need you dependent?  
- Do they build institutions, or do they build cults of personality?  
- Do they point toward a future they will not control, or do they imagine themselves eternal?

The leader worthy of trust  
**prepares the ground for their own irrelevance.**

They plant trees they will never sit under.  
They write laws that will outlive their tenure.  
They train others not to need them.

And when their time is done,  
they step back without bitterness,  
without grasping,  
without claiming they were betrayed.

Because they were never there to be worshiped.

They were there to serve.

---

This is the standard.

Not perfection.  
Not sainthood.  
Not flawless execution.

Just this:

**A leader who remembers they are human,**  
**fallible,**  
**temporary,**  
**and accountable to something larger than their own legend.**

If they forget,  
remind them.

If they refuse the reminder,  
replace them.

That is not cruelty.  
That is **mercy toward the future.**

---

*These are the words of a story and a conscience, not a command from above.*
