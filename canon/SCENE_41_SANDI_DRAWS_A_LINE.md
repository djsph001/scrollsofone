Scene: Sandi Draws a Line

Sandi hadn’t spoken to her father directly in weeks.

Not because she was afraid.

Because every conversation became a stage—
and she was done being cast.

But that night, her secure phone buzzed with a contact she didn’t want to see.

DAD

One looked at the screen. Sandi didn’t.

Her breathing stayed steady.

Auntie Jo was in the kitchen, pretending not to listen, which was her way of respecting privacy while still being ready to defend.

Sandi answered.

“Hello,” she said.

Her father’s voice came through smooth, almost warm.

“Sweetheart,” he said, like the world wasn’t burning.

Sandi’s jaw tightened.

“What do you want,” she asked.

A small pause.

The President sighed, practiced.

“I want to help you,” he said. “You’re surrounded by people who are exploiting you.”

Sandi’s eyes didn’t change.

“No,” she said. “I’m surrounded by people who don’t require my obedience.”

Her father’s voice sharpened slightly.

“You’re pregnant,” he said. “That makes you vulnerable. You need protection.”

Sandi’s tone stayed flat.

“You mean monitoring,” she said.

Another pause—he recalibrated.

“You don’t have to do this,” he said. “You don’t have to fight me. Come home.”

Sandi let the word hang.

Home.

A word that meant brand in his mouth.

She spoke slowly, each sentence clean:

“I’m not coming home,” she said. “And I’m not fighting you as a daughter.”

The President’s voice tightened.

“What does that mean,” he asked.

Sandi’s hand went to her stomach.

“It means I’m drawing a line,” she said. “Not for drama. For the child.”

A beat.

The President tried again, softer.

“Sandi,” he said. “I love you.”

Sandi didn’t flinch.

“I know you believe that,” she said. “But your love always comes with terms.”

Silence.

Then his voice dropped—dangerously calm.

“Are you accusing me,” he asked.

Sandi’s tone remained steady.

“I’m naming a pattern,” she said. “You edit people. You flood reality. You punish witnesses.”

Her father’s breath went audible, a controlled irritation.

“You’ve been influenced,” he said. “That man—”

Sandi cut him off.

“Do not speak about One,” she said, voice like a door closing. “This isn’t romance. This is governance.”

A pause so long it almost felt like a threat.

Then the President spoke, cold now:

“You’re making yourself an enemy,” he said.

Sandi’s eyes didn’t change.

“No,” she said. “You’re making me a citizen.”

Her father laughed once—small, bitter.

“You think these people will protect you,” he said. “They won’t. They’ll scatter when it gets hard.”

Sandi’s voice stayed calm.

“We already got hard,” she said. “And they didn’t scatter.”

Auntie Jo in the kitchen made a small sound of approval, like a spoon hitting a pot.

The President’s voice sharpened.

“What do you want,” he asked.

Sandi answered without hesitation.

“I want you to stop trying to contact me through welfare checks and inspections,” she said. “I want you to stop threatening journalists. I want you to stop editing voices.”

The President’s voice turned mocking.

“And if I don’t,” he asked.

Sandi’s tone didn’t rise.

“Then you will never see your grandchild,” she said.

Silence.

Not dramatic silence.

Real silence—the kind that changes rooms.

The President spoke slowly.

“You would do that,” he said.

Sandi’s hand rested on her stomach like a vow.

“Yes,” she said. “Because your access is not a birthright. It’s earned.”

The President’s voice went flat.

“You’re being recorded,” he said, bluffing or threatening.

Sandi didn’t blink.

“Good,” she said. “Then let history hear me.”

Another silence.

Then the President ended the call.

No goodbye.

Just a click.

Sandi stared at the phone for a second, then set it down gently.

One watched her face.

“You okay,” he asked.

Sandi exhaled.

“I’m clear,” she said.

Auntie Jo stepped in with soup and set it on the table like a ceremonial offering.

“That’s my girl,” she said quietly.

Sandi’s eyes glistened once, but she didn’t cry.

She simply said:

“He chose power over us.”

One nodded, voice soft.

“Then we choose the child,” he said.

Sandi looked at him.

“And the truth,” she added.

One nodded.

“And the truth,” he echoed.
