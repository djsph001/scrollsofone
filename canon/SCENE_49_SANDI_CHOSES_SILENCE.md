Scene: The Silence

The next morning, Sandi’s name trended again.

Not because she posted.

Because they made her a prop.

A viral influencer did a skit:

A fake “First Daughter” clutching her belly, whispering:

“Tonight’s circle is canceled… because someone used punctuation.”

Millions of views.

Comments full of laughing emojis and cruelty disguised as comedy.

People tagging Sandi as if she was entertainment.

One watched it once, then closed the app.

Sandi didn’t watch it at all.

She sat at the kitchen table with a notebook and wrote down three things:
	1.	Doctor appointment schedule
	2.	Safe housing backups
	3.	Who gets access to the baby

Auntie Jo poured tea and watched her with quiet respect.

“You’re not going to respond,” Auntie Jo said.

Sandi shook her head.

“No,” she replied. “Not to that.”

One looked at her.

“You’re angry,” he said gently.

Sandi nodded.

“Yes,” she said. “But I’m not giving them my nervous system.”

She wrote a fourth item:
	4.	No content about the baby. Ever.

One exhaled.

“That’s hard,” he said. “People will demand it.”

Sandi’s eyes stayed steady.

“Then they’ll be disappointed,” she said. “Disappointment is not my emergency.”

She slid the notebook toward One.

“What do you want to do,” she asked.

One stared at the page.

He wanted to speak.

He wanted to defend her.

He wanted to scorch the comedians with truth until their jokes turned to ash.

Then he remembered the rule he wrote:

Truth without spectacle.

He pushed the notebook back.

“We protect the circles,” he said. “We protect Keisha. We protect the hosts.”

Sandi nodded.

“And we let them laugh,” she said. “Because the laugh is covering fear.”

Auntie Jo sipped tea.

“Let them laugh,” she said. “We’ll still be here when the joke gets old.”

Sandi looked at One.

“I’m choosing silence,” she said. “Not because I’m weak. Because I’m refusing to be trained.”

One nodded slowly.

“That’s Fourth Branch,” he said. “That’s the culture.”

Sandi’s hand rested on her stomach.

“And that,” she said, “is motherhood.”
