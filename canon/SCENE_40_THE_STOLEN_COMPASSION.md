Scene: The Stolen Compassion

The regime didn’t attack the Mutual Aid Branch head-on.

It tried to absorb it.

That’s how empires survive:
they swallow what threatens them and rebrand it as their idea.

A week after the witness circles began supporting each other, a polished announcement appeared from a new White House initiative:

THE COMMUNITY STABILITY PROGRAM
Support for Families Impacted by Online Harassment and “Misinformation Panic.”

The language was syrup-smooth.
	•	“mental wellness resources”
	•	“community safety liaisons”
	•	“rapid response support”
	•	“trusted information partners”

To the average viewer, it sounded kind.

Even noble.

But Helen read it like a librarian reads fine print:

Not as words.

As structure.

She forwarded it to the circle with a two-word note:

It’s a trap.

In the small room, Sandi read it too and felt her spine stiffen.

“They’re trying to make care conditional,” she said.

One nodded.

“Care that requires allegiance is not care,” he replied. “It’s recruitment.”

Auntie Jo laughed, sharp.

“They want to help you,” she said, stirring soup, “by asking for your name.”

Helen convened an emergency circle—quiet, invite-only.

She put the program printout on the table and said:

“Let’s do what we always do.”

No panic.

Method.

They applied the same checklist they used for clips:
	•	Who runs it?
	•	What do they collect?
	•	What happens if you refuse?
	•	Who benefits?

A lawyer volunteer—Miguel—tapped the page.

“This is surveillance wrapped in services,” he said. “They’ll collect identities, locations, social ties. Then they’ll ‘support’ you by mapping you.”

Ms. Rivera swallowed.

“It’s tempting,” she admitted. “If you’re scared and broke, you’ll take anything.”

Helen nodded.

“That’s why it’s designed,” she said. “It doesn’t have to fool the strong. It has to catch the exhausted.”

The veteran grunted.

“So what’s the counter,” he asked.

Helen looked at One.

Not for a speech.

For a rule.

One answered calmly.

“We respond the same way,” he said. “No outrage. No attacks. Just a public warning in plain language: what it collects, what it costs, how to refuse safely.”

Sandi added, voice hard:

“And we make our aid easier to access than theirs,” she said. “Because people will choose the path that keeps their kids fed.”

Miguel nodded.

“Also,” he said, “we draft a one-page guide: ‘How to accept help without surrendering your data.’ People need options.”

Helen wrote it down:

AID WITHOUT IDENTIFICATION
HELP WITHOUT CAPTURE

One looked at the paper again.

“They’re stealing compassion,” he said quietly.

Sandi’s eyes narrowed.

“No,” she corrected. “They’re stealing the appearance of compassion.”

A pause.

“And they can’t keep it,” she added. “Not without changing.”

That was the deeper threat:

If the regime truly offered unconditional support, it would stop being the regime.

So it couldn’t.

It could only counterfeit care the way it counterfeited voices.
